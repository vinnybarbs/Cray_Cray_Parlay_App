## Per-candidate analysis

### TheTell — 4/5
SERP for "TheTell" is fragmented: Amy Griffin's 2025 memoir *The Tell*, the Dara Horn-founded Tell Institute, the TELL storytelling app, and references to Poe's "Tell-Tale Heart." TheTell.com is listed at PerfectDomain as "may be for sale." No incumbent dominates with Wikipedia-class authority; the memoir is the strongest signal but books decay in SERP over 12-18 months. Sports-betting query surfaces zero competitors. "The tell" is also core poker/betting vocabulary (a behavioral signal) — categorically on-thesis. A new product with sustained content and brand SEO should be able to rank #1 for "TheTell sports" or "TheTell picks" inside 6-12 months, and could plausibly compete for the bare name within 18 months.

### Surfaced — 4/5
SERP is split between the Cambridge dictionary entry, "Surfaced" — a Kentucky metalcore band (2022) — and Surfaced Clothing. There's also a YC-style AEO tool at usesurfaced.com but it's tiny. No Wikipedia-class incumbent. Sports-betting query returns the generic sportsbook listicles; nothing called Surfaced exists in betting. The .com appears to be the band on Bandcamp/Instagram (@surfacedhc) rather than a corporate site. "Surfaced" reads as on-brand for "we surface picks/edges for you" and SERP is wide open for a category entrant. Realistic to dominate within 12 months with normal SEO.

### Marked — 2/5
This is dangerous. Multiple Marked products exist: Marked.app (the popular Mac markdown previewer), Marked 2, Marked.js (developer tool with strong GitHub presence), Netflix's "Marked" series (2025), AND — critically — **MarkedSports.com already exists** offering "backtested and proven advanced predictive stats, with picks, algorithm projections, and bankroll management strategies." That's a near-identical positioning to our product on the matching .com. SERP for "Marked sports betting picks" surfaces that exact competitor as the top organic result. Even if MarkedSports is small, they own the keyword combination we'd need. Outranking a direct-category incumbent on a generic dictionary word is the Trapline pattern. Drop.

### TheRead — 1/5
*The Read* is a Wikipedia-documented podcast hosted by Kid Fury and Crissle, running since March 2013, peaking #1 in iTunes comedy and #3 New & Noteworthy, distributed via SiriusXM Podcasts+, with a 12+ year audience. It has a website (thisistheread.com), 10K+ Instagram, Apple Podcasts ID 619369512, and a Wikipedia entry. This is the Trapline scenario exactly: a culturally-load-bearing incumbent in the broad "content/media" category with audience overlap (young, internet-native, content-consuming) to our exact ICP. We would be invisible behind it. Drop.

### TheMove — 2/5
THEMOVE is Lance Armstrong's cycling podcast on WEDŪ, with Momentous as a major sponsor, multi-year run, dedicated Apple Podcasts listing, and a parallel "The Move Studio" fitness brand. Critically, it's *sports-adjacent* (endurance cycling) and discusses Tour de France racing — adjacent enough that crossover SEO matters. "Pat McAfee's move to ESPN" headlines further pollute the SERP. "The move" is also generic betting slang ("what's the move?"), so brand-class qualifiers won't disambiguate cleanly. We'd be the third or fourth sports-adjacent "Move" property. Drop or downgrade.

### Steamline — 2/5
SteamLine Luggage (founded 2008 by Sara Banks, Dublin) has 16+ years of SEO authority, Anthropology distribution, Reese Witherspoon and Margot Robbie press placements, and ranks for both "Steamline" and "Steamline luggage." This is the **textbook Trapline pattern** — a niche-but-authoritative consumer brand has owned this word for ~16 years. Even though "steam" is core sharp-betting vocabulary (steam moves, line steam), our product would be permanently behind a luggage company in SERP. The very lesson v1 was supposed to teach. Drop.

### TheLine — 1/5
This is the worst SERP collision of the 12. "TheLine" is dominated by Saudi Arabia's NEOM mega-project (Wikipedia entry, ongoing CNBC/Euronews/Britannica news cycle, sustained press 2022-2026), The LINE Hotels (boutique chain in LA/DC/Austin), Khaite at theline.com (high-fashion brand), THE LINE animation studio in London, and the 2023 indie film. Every single one of these has more SEO authority than a new sports app could plausibly accrue. Also, in betting, "the line" is the most generic possible term — every sportsbook page targets it. Triple penalty: massive incumbents, generic vocab, polluted SERP. Drop.

### Tape — 1/5
"Tape" is a generic English word with 3M/Scotch Tape, Home Depot, Uline, multiple competing apps named Tape (cassette emulator, audio recording, events platform). Worse: **Sporttrade's app has a feature called "Tape"** — direct in-category collision with a $-backed, Nasdaq/Comcast-affiliated betting product. We would be both fighting a generic dictionary word and an active competitor's named feature. Drop.

### Tabular — 2/5
The SERP is locked up by tech: Tabular Editor 3 (Power BI tool), Tabular.email, and **Tabular Inc — the Apache Iceberg founders' company acquired by Databricks in June 2024 for an unconfirmed but reportedly ~$1B+ figure**. The acquisition generated sustained tech press in 2024-2025. Microsoft Research also has a project called Tabular. While none of this is sports-related (good for intent), the SERP authority stack is enterprise-tier — Databricks, VentureBeat, Microsoft. "Tabular" also doesn't reference betting at all; the brand would have to fight for relevance on a word that means "in a table." Drop or hold.

### Marker — 2/5
Marker is a globally established ski-bindings brand (founded Germany 1952), Sharpie/Expo dominate stationery, datalab-to/marker is a popular GitHub PDF→markdown tool, and Marker was a Medium publication (now defunct but still SEO-resident). Skiing is *sports* — sports retailers and gear sites pollute the SERP. No betting collision specifically, but the path to ranking on the bare name is blocked by a 73-year-old brand. We could possibly rank for "marker picks" or "marker betting" but not for "marker." Drop or downgrade.

### Gauge — 3/5
Distributed competition: Gauge.ai (consumer feedback), withgauge.com (YC-backed AEO tool, 2024), gaugeonline.com (certification testing), Gauge.org (open-source test framework), Gauge Microphones, Gauge car-selling. Critically, **gauge.com is parked at "Coming Soon"** — the prime domain is unclaimed. No single incumbent is Wikipedia-class. The word also resonates with our value prop ("gauge the matchup"). Sports-betting search returns zero collisions. Realistic path: coexist with brand-class qualifier ("Gauge picks", "Gauge betting") and capture the bare name as our content authority grows. Borderline 3-4; advance with caveats.

### Fadeline — 5/5
The cleanest SERP in the audit. Top results: a tiny Obsidian plugin on GitHub (single-developer project), Fadeline Signature font from a Banda Aceh designer, FadeLines Barber Shop, fadeline.com appears unclaimed. No Wikipedia entry, no Fortune 500 brand, no app store contender, no sports overlap, no podcast, no media. AND "fade" is core sharp-betting vocabulary (fading the public, fade the chalk), so the name is on-thesis. This is the rare case where we could plausibly rank #1 for the bare name within 6 months of launch with minimal SEO investment. Strongest viability of the 12.

## Recommendation: SEO survivors

**Advance to Phase 4 (5/5 and 4/5 — clean SERPs):**
1. **Fadeline** (5/5) — wide-open SERP, on-brand sharp-betting vocab, no incumbent of any meaningful size
2. **TheTell** (4/5) — fragmented incumbent, .com for sale, "tell" is poker-thesis vocabulary
3. **Surfaced** (4/5) — only a small metalcore band and clothing brand to coexist with

**Advance with caveats (3/5 — needs brand-class qualifier strategy):**
4. **Gauge** (3/5) — distributed mid-size competition; .com is parked. We'd need to consistently brand as "Gauge picks" / "Gauge app" in launch content; coexisting is realistic, dominating the bare name is multi-year.

**Drop (1-2/5 — Trapline-class or in-category collisions):**
- **TheRead** (1/5) — Wikipedia-class podcast incumbent in adjacent media space
- **TheLine** (1/5) — NEOM, hotels, fashion, film, and most generic betting term in English
- **Tape** (1/5) — Sporttrade owns a feature called Tape; 3M generic
- **Marked** (2/5) — MarkedSports.com is a direct competitor with the matching .com already shipping picks
- **TheMove** (2/5) — Lance Armstrong's podcast in adjacent sports vertical
- **Steamline** (2/5) — exact Trapline pattern (16-year DTC luggage authority on the bare word)
- **Tabular** (2/5) — Databricks-owned enterprise data brand
- **Marker** (2/5) — 73-year-old ski brand, sports-adjacent SERP
