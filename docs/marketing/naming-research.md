**Pattern A takeaways:**
- Word count: ~75% single-word or single-compound; ~25% two-word.
- Letter count: 5–10 letters dominates. Outliers (Establish The Run, SharpEdge Analytics) are publisher-style, not app-style.
- Tone: split between **quant-coded** (Outlier, Unabated, Stokastic, Rithmm, EV Analytics) and **in-group slang** (Dimers, Sharp, Juice Reel, RotoGrinders). Almost nothing in the middle.
- Most over-fished morphemes: **"Sharp"** (10+ extant brands), **"Edge"** (BettorEdge, SharpEdge, EdgeSports, EdgHouse, PropsEdge, Sharp Edge Tech, EdgeAnalytics, etc.), **"Roto-"** (legacy fantasy).
- White space inside Pattern A: **invented one-word names with math/quant coding** that are NOT "Sharp" or "Edge" derivatives. Rithmm, Stokastic, Outlier, and Unabated occupy this lane but it's not crowded yet.

## Section 2 — Audience vocabulary inventory

Words that recur across the sources below. Most are paraphrased from glossary snippets unless quoted. Verbatim source examples cited.

### Acts of betting
1. **Hammer** — "to place a large or aggressive bet on something" (paraphrased from glossary snippets; canonical r/sportsbook usage)
2. **Fade** — **"to bet against a team, player, or trend"** [glossary snippet, multiple sources]
3. **Tail** — **"to tail another bettor's picks"** = follow them [BetMGM glossary]
4. **Smash** — used like "hammer," r/sportsbook canonical
5. **Lean** — **"an initial gut feeling about the outcome before all the information... has been fully considered"** [glossary snippet]
6. **Play** — generic verb for "make a bet" (e.g. "I'm playing the under")
7. **Bang** / **pound** — variants of hammer, slightly less common
8. **Sprinkle** — small bet, often a long-shot prop
9. **Run it back** — re-bet after a loss

### Edges / value
10. **Edge** — overused (10+ brands in category). Canonical, but no longer distinctive.
11. **+EV / +ev** — **"positive expected value betting"** [OddsJam education snippet] — quant-coded
12. **Value** — generic; commonly paired with "spot the value" or "value play"
13. **Juice / Vig** — **"the commission that a sportsbook charges for taking a bet"** [SI snippet]; canonical
14. **No juice** — **"a bet with no juice or vig means you win exactly what you risk"** [glossary snippet]
15. **The number** / **the line** / **the price** — interchangeable terms for the posted spread/odds
16. **Mispriced** — sharp-curious vocabulary; less popular but signals quant tribe
17. **CLV (Closing Line Value)** — sharp-curious shorthand

### Picks
18. **Pick** — generic, neutral
19. **Play** — also functions as a noun ("today's plays")
20. **Lock** — **"a sure thing or a bet that's almost guaranteed to win"** [glossary snippet] — note: Cray's brand explicitly rejects this word
21. **Smash** — both verb and noun
22. **Lean** — also a noun ("my lean is the under")
23. **Dime** — origin of "Dimers"; a $1k bet, used as a confidence signal
24. **The chalk** — heavy favorite; **"What Does 'The Chalk' Mean"** [Action Network education page]

### Self-identification
25. **Degen / Degenerate** — **"a compulsive gambler with unhealthy habits"** [glossary snippet]; used self-referentially in r/sportsbook
26. **Sharp** — **"a 'smart' bettor who is consistently successful"** [glossary snippet]; the most-loaded positive identifier
27. **Wiseguy** — old-school synonym for sharp
28. **Square** — **"a new bettor without a lot of experience"** [glossary snippet]; pejorative
29. **Capper / Handicapper** — **"people that study and wager on sports events"** [Elite Pickz]; neutral
30. **Tout** — **"a person or company that sells their expert opinion"** [Doc Sports] — note: **negative connotation**, "almost always means a hard, aggressive selling style coupled with exaggerated and even false claims" [paraphrased]. Cray must NOT brand near "tout."

### Outcomes
31. **Hit** — won the bet
32. **Cash / Cashed** — won; also "cash out"
33. **Bag / Bag secured** — won big
34. **Smoked / Got smoked** — lost badly
35. **Bad beat** — **"a bad luck loss, usually because something unexpected happens at the end of a game"** [glossary snippet]
36. **Down bad** — losing streak, broader internet slang adopted by degens
37. **Ruin** — long-term losing

**Section 2 strategic notes:**
- "Edge" is over-saturated. Branding on it == invisibility.
- "Sharp" is the most-loved positive identifier but is owned by 10+ brands.
- **"Lean," "play," "lock"** are the most-used neutral vocabulary across both ICPs. "Lock" is poisoned for Cray's brand (already a hard-no per `product-marketing-context.md` §10).
- **"Fade" and "Trap"** are the wedge-adjacent words. Cray's existing "Trap" label sits in genuinely under-claimed territory. "Fade" is the audience's word for the same action.
- **"The number"** is interesting vocabulary nobody has claimed as a product name — sharp-coded, neutral, repeatable.

## Section 4 — Strategic recommendations

### Where the white space is

1. **Single-word, invented-or-repurposed, math-coded names** — the Rithmm / Stokastic / Outlier lane. Crowded but not saturated. A new entry here would feel native to the category to both ICPs.

2. **Audience-vocabulary names that are NOT "Edge" or "Sharp"** — the under-claimed slang: "Fade," "Trap," "Lean," "Hammer," "Dime," "Chalk," "Number." Cray's existing wedge already encodes "Trap." Building the brand around that exact word is a defensible position because no major product owns it.

3. **The Robinhood archetype for betting** — a folkloric protagonist name that frames the product as fighting on the user's side against the books. Currently uncontested. Highest-risk / highest-reward.

### Where the over-fishing is

- **Anything with "Edge"** — 10+ active brands. Invisibility risk.
- **Anything with "Sharp"** — 10+ active brands (Sharp.app, SharpEdge, SharpRank, SharpSide, SharpSports, SharpHunter, etc.). Saturation.
- **Anything with "Bet" prefix** — owned by sportsbooks. Wrong category signal.
- **"Roto" prefix** — legacy fantasy, wrong era.

### Archetypes worth generating against (in priority order)

1. **In-group vocabulary, single word, untainted morpheme** (Trap, Fade, Lean, Chalk, Dime, Number). Pratfall-effect compatible. Sharp-curious read it as analytical insider; degens read it as tribe. Avoids "Lock" and "Tout" hard-nos.
2. **Invented or math-rooted single word, 6–8 letters** (Stokastic / Rithmm / Outlier lane). Sharp-curious-first; degens will tolerate it because Vince's voice carries the degen register elsewhere in product.
3. **Two-word compound where one word is in-group slang + one is analytical** (e.g. `[slang]+[analytical]` — the Juice Reel / SharpRank pattern). Most directly serves dual-ICP.
4. **Folkloric protagonist (Robinhood archetype)** — high-risk, requires brand investment to seat. Worth one or two candidates but not the bulk of the list.

### Anti-patterns confirmed by this research

- Three-word names (Establish The Run pattern) — only works for content/podcast brands. Cray is a product.
- Names with "Parlay" / "Spread" / "ML" — bet-type lock-in (already in framework anti-pattern list).
- "Sharp" or "Edge" anywhere — invisibility.
- "BET" suffix — wrong category.
- Misspellings for vibes (Bettr, Wynnd) — already framework-disqualified.
