# Naming Framework — Cray Cray for Parlays Rename Decision

**Status:** draft for review · 2026-05-12
**Purpose:** Apply naming-psychology principles to the rename reality-check. Any candidate name (including "keep current") gets audited against this. Pairs with the market-research findings (`naming-research.md`, pending) to anchor the brainstorm in evidence, not instinct.

---

## 1. The job the name needs to do

Per Jobs-to-Be-Done framing, a product name is "hired" to perform specific jobs. Cray Cray's name needs to:

| Job | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **Signal analytical rigor in <1 second** | Sharp-curious ICP (50% of audience) won't read past a name that signals vibes |
| **Be memorable + repeatable in conversation** | Both ICPs share via word-of-mouth and Twitter — names that aren't sayable die |
| **Hint at the wedge (negative edges / Trap)** | The differentiator should ideally be encoded in the name itself, or at minimum compatible with it |
| **Not box the product into one market type** | Today's product covers ML / spread / total / props. "Parlays" in the name now lies about scope |
| **Work as URL + spoken + Twitter handle + app icon** | Five surfaces must all work |
| **Survive a +5-year horizon** | Pivots cost backlinks, brand equity, app store rankings |
| **Pass the legal sniff (no trademark collision)** | Sports betting has heavy trademark activity |
| **Have a clean .com or .app domain available** | Cute alternative TLDs hurt trust signals in this category |

A name that fails ANY of the top four jobs is a structural liability, not a stylistic choice.

## 2. Psychology applied to naming

From the marketing-psychology skill, these models apply directly to naming. Each has an implication for our shortlist:

### Mere Exposure Effect
*"People prefer things they've seen before."*

→ Names that pattern-match to other trustworthy products in adjacent categories get a free familiarity bump. "Cray" already has equity for Vince; that equity is a real asset. But "Cray Cray" patterns to *podcast brands*, not analytical products — and that's the tension we're solving.

### Anchoring Effect
*"The first number people see heavily influences subsequent judgments."*

→ The first word a visitor reads anchors their interpretation of everything that follows. A name starting with "Cray Cray" anchors degen-vibes; the user then has to *override* that anchor to take the math seriously. A name starting with an analytical word reverses the anchor: math first, irreverence as a layered surprise.

### Framing Effect
*"Same facts, different frames."*

→ Same product, different name = different category in the user's head. "Cray Cray for Parlays" frames us as a parlay tool. "Cray Cray Edge" frames us as an edge product. The product hasn't changed; the perception has.

### Authority Bias
*"People defer to experts and authority figures."*

→ Analytically-coded names (Edge / Sharp / Signal / Quant) trigger authority signals. The hard question: do we WANT that, or does the dual-audience product benefit from defying expectations?

### Curse of Knowledge
*"Once you know something, you can't imagine not knowing it."*

→ Vince has lived with "Cray Cray" for months. The name FEELS obvious to him. To a new visitor, it doesn't read as "wild because the math is sharp, just like our brand" — it reads as wild, full stop. We can't trust our own ear.

### Lindy Effect
*"The longer something has survived, the longer it's likely to continue."*

→ Analytical-product naming patterns (proper noun, function, or compound math-word) have decades of survival. Joke-named products in serious categories rarely outlast their initial wave. Bet against the joke.

### Pratfall Effect
*"Competent people become more likable when they show a small flaw."*

→ A degen-coded name on a deeply analytical product COULD be the deliberate pratfall — *"we're so confident in the math that we can afford to be cheeky."* This is the only argument that defends "Cray Cray" for the long haul. It only works if everything else (aesthetic, copy, proof, pricing) is dead serious. Which the just-shipped Landing genuinely is.

### Unity Principle
*"Shared identity drives influence. 'One of us' is powerful."*

→ Does the name signal "one of us" to the ICP? "Cray Cray" signals tribe-membership to degens — they recognize the self-aware irony. Sharp-curious bettors don't have a single tribe vocabulary, but they DO have category words: edge, sharp, model, signal, line, value, vig. Names that include these signal in-group.

### Hick's Law
*"More options = slower decisions."*

→ We need to brainstorm broadly, then narrow ruthlessly. A shortlist of 20 candidates is paralysis. We end with 3-5 named-and-scored, no more.

### Decoy Effect (for our own decision process)
*"A third inferior option makes one of the original two look better."*

→ Watch for this in our own evaluation. If I show Vince three options, my own bias toward "Cray Cray Edge" could plant the other two as visible decoys. Defense: ensure every shortlist candidate is a genuine contender, not a foil.

## 3. What to AVOID (anti-patterns)

| Anti-pattern | Example | Why it fails |
|---|---|---|
| Two-word jokes | Cray Cray, Bet Buddy, Lock Doctor | Reads as podcast/blog, not product |
| Bet-type lock-in | ParlayKing, SpreadBox | Boxes future product expansion |
| Generic noun stacks | BetLab, OddsCore | Indistinguishable from competitors |
| Misspellings for vibes | Bettr, Wynnd, Lynks | Bad for SEO + bad for verbal share |
| Hard-to-pronounce | Zynqr, Qbet | Mere exposure breaks if people can't say it |
| Trademark-fragile | Anything Vegas/Sportsbook + adjective | Already crowded |
| Negative connotation | TrapHouse, BadBet, LossLeader | Misreads as anti-bettor |
| .io / .gg / .xyz only | (forced by .com unavailability) | Reads as crypto-scam in this category |

## 4. Criteria checklist (each candidate must pass)

A candidate name must pass ALL of the below to advance to scoring:

- [ ] Pronounceable in <2 seconds by a stranger
- [ ] Spellable from hearing it spoken
- [ ] Searchable on Google (returns the product, not generic noise)
- [ ] `.com` or `.app` available at reasonable cost
- [ ] Twitter / X handle available (or close variant)
- [ ] No trademark collision in sports betting / analytics (TESS quick check)
- [ ] Compatible with the villain-frame positioning ("which side is the trap")
- [ ] Works for both ICPs (degen + sharp-curious) — not exclusive to one
- [ ] No "parlay" lock-in
- [ ] Survives 5-year horizon (no time-period words, no trend words)

---

## 5. Scoring matrix

After the criteria checklist, surviving candidates get scored on five dimensions:

| Dimension | Weight | What 5/5 looks like |
|---|---|---|
| **Memorability** | 25% | Repeats easily, sticks after one hearing |
| **Brand-fit to wedge** | 25% | Encodes "math + negative edges" without explaining |
| **Audience-fit (both ICPs)** | 20% | Degens read it as in-group, sharp-curious read it as authoritative |
| **Pronounceability** | 15% | Stranger can pronounce + spell first try |
| **Domain + handle availability** | 15% | .com + Twitter clean |

Total = 100 points. Scores below 75 are auto-disqualified. Top 3 advance to Vince's pick.
